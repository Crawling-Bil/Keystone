### Topology Builder 2.1: a lone end point stands beside its switch, access points are not guessed onto a second switch, fewer lines on top of each other

- **A PC, phone or small access-point frame far from its switch.**  In the one-diagram view a row that holds the end points of several switches could leave a single PC many icon steps from the switch it is plugged into, so its line crossed half the page (in the all-floor run of one estate a PC stood far from its switch, so its line crossed other lines).  A second pass (`layout._bring_lone_ends`) takes the lone end points, and frames of at most three members, that stand more than 6 icon steps from their switch and puts each at the nearest free boundary of its row, never between two members of one frame.  The row is then spread again with the frames as units (`_spread_framed`), so the order inside a frame and the comb channels do not change.
- **Access points are not drawn on two switches.**  A port description such as `AP-3F-02` guessed a cable to an access point that another switch already reports by CDP/LLDP, so the access point had two attachments and could not stand in a frame.  A cable that a neighbour protocol reported wins; the stale description is no longer drawn and is listed in TOPO-028 ("port description(s) name another device than the one the port faces, or a device that reports itself on another switch").  Only access points and phones are treated as single-homed; a server or a switch can legitimately appear twice.
- **Hypervisor hosts and Linux servers are servers.**  Linux bridges advertise Bridge, WLAN, Router and Station together, so ESXi, KVM, XCP-ng, oVirt and plain Linux hosts read as access points.  The description rules now recognise them (VMware ESXi, Proxmox, XCP-ng, oVirt, KVM, libvirt, Ubuntu / Debian / Red Hat family, Windows Server, FreeBSD) and UniFi access points (`UAP-`, `U6-`, `UniFi AP`, `EAP###`).
- **Frames keep their lanes.**  The pitch of the lanes of an end-point frame comes from its full lines, not from the short last line whose half-step offsets shrank the strip and put lines through the names of neighbours.
- **Two long bridges on one bearing.**  Two guessed cables from one core switch to two neighbour-floor switches that lie on the same bearing were drawn on top of each other for 2,275 px; the later bridge now takes the next sideways offset when it would ride on a routed line and the new route adds no crossing.
- **Long loops round one device.**  A long line whose straight path is blocked by one switch standing almost on it was routed as a bridge, a sideways copy of the whole line, and made a loop round the lower rows (3,300 px for a 2,500 px link on a floor page with 25 neighbour switches).  When the routed line is more than 300 px longer than the straight one, a single bend that clears every device, on either side and as close to the straight line as possible, replaces it, if it is at least 150 px shorter, lies on no other line and crosses no more lines.
- **Measured** on 22 saved runs (265 pages) against 2.0: collinear overlay 32,033 px to 25,753, crossings 1,353 to 1,251, access points with two attachments 42 to 0, access points outside their frame 16 to 10; no page has more crossings, icons crossed or title hits.  On the 20 real packs only the four packs of one estate change (two guessed access-point cables became a notice); the other 16 are identical.
- Tests: `tests/test_topology_v21.py` (31), invented names only.  Documentation: IEEE 802.1AB https://1.ieee802.org/maintenance/p802-1ab-2016-revision-station-and-media-access-control-connectivity-discovery/ ; Cisco LLDP configuration https://www.cisco.com/c/en/us/td/docs/switches/lan/c9000/lyr2-fwd/cdp-lldp-mac-udld/cdp-lldp-mac-udld-configuration-guide/configure-lldp.html ; VMware LLDP on a distributed switch https://techdocs.broadcom.com/us/en/vmware-cis/vsphere/vsphere/7-0/vsphere-networking/monitoring-network-packets/switch-discovery-protocol/enable-link-layer-discovery-protocol-on-a-vsphere-distributed-switch.html

### Topology Builder 2.0: a neighbour switch no longer stays far from its parent when it shares a row with a switch that was moved

- **The cause.**  In the one-diagram view, when a row was moved to bring a child switch under its parent, the other devices of that row (a leaf, an access point host, a second switch) had no wish of their own and were packed against the device before them.  A leaf could therefore be carried 7 to 8 icon steps away from its parent although the parent had room next to it, which is what the last two screenshots show.  Now every other device of such a row keeps the place the sweeps gave it, and only the device that has to follow its parent moves.
- **A parent may pull two switches, not one.**  `FOLLOW_KIDS` goes from 1 to 2: a switch with at most two switches under it pulls them under itself in rows that are only looked at for this.  Three was tried and rejected (it collapses rows in the random-frame tests); more has no measurable gain.
- **Measured.**  The longest parent-to-leaf line of the tam-allsite-1diagram run was 3,450 px and is now adjacent.  In 1,000 invented estates the leaves farther than 6 icon steps from their parent go from 15 to 10, those farther than 7 from 6 to 3, and the farthest from 9.3 to 8.1 steps.  22 runs of your exports: crossings 1,485, lines through devices 48, overlay 32,033, lines through frame titles 221 (all unchanged, no page worse); lines through device names 4,473 to 4,457.  Stored packs: 60 layouts and the findings of 20 packs unchanged; full suite 4,403 passed, 22 skipped.
- Tests: `tests/test_topology_v20.py` (6), invented names only.

### Topology Builder 1.9: a switch seen only by its neighbours comes back to its parent in every row, port-channels show their member ports, and a hypervisor host is a server

Found on seven new runs of 1.8 (a multi-site estate of one head office and several branches, a single-site HRGA set and a data-centre WAN set; "One diagram" and "Auto").  In the one-diagram Overview of a multi-site estate an access switch that nobody captured (`PDC-DAYA-SWAC-03-296`, seen only by the LLDP of its parent) stood 3,826 px from its parent, with a long grey line across the whole page.  1.8 only brought children back in rows that hold no frame, and the fit of a row that holds frames (the order of the row is kept) pushed the child to the far end.

- **A child follows its parent in every row (`layout._arrange.centre_owners`).**  The follow rule of 1.8 is now looked at in every row, not only in rows that hold no frame owner.  A switch (`SWITCH_ROLES`, captured or only seen, or anything with something under it) that stands more than `FOLLOW_FAR` (6 icon steps) from the mean position of the switches above it that it is cabled to takes the place in the row that its parent's x gives it (a row keeps its order, so it is moved in the order, not only in x; devices of an HA pair stay together).  A second look is taken at the fit of the first, because the fit itself (frames packed apart) is what pushes a child away.  In a row that is only looked at for this, a parent with more than `FOLLOW_KIDS` (1) switch under it does not pull them: a core would otherwise pull its whole row of access switches onto itself.  Rows that 1.8 moved anyway keep the 1.8 rule (`FOLLOW_ALL`).
- **Port-channels and Eth-Trunks show their member ports (new option, on by default).**  A line used to name the bundle at the end where the switch had captured it (`Po12`) and the physical ports only at an end that had no bundle (`Gi0/0/23, Gi0/0/24`), so the engineer had to open the Bundles table to read which ports carry the channel.  Now the end reads `Po12: Gi1/0/5, Gi2/0/5`, `Po2: Gi0/1-2`, `Eth-Trunk3: GE1/0/22, GE2/0/22`: consecutive ports of one card are joined (`Gi1/0/23-24`), a list longer than 34 characters becomes `20 members` (the tooltip and the Bundles table keep the full list), and the other end of the line uses the same short form.  The option is "Show the member ports of port-channels and Eth-Trunks" on the Topology Builder page (`bundle_members` in the form, `run-settings.txt` and the history); with it off the pictures are those of 1.8.  Implementation: `labels.member_ports`, `labels.annotate_members` fills `Connection.members_a` / `members_b`, `labels.end_texts` writes them; SVG, draw.io and label placement all use `end_texts`, so the three agree.  Layout and crossings are unchanged (18 runs: same crossings, overlay and lines through devices; port labels over device names 118 to 112).
- **A host that advertises Bridge, WLAN, Router and Station is a server (`roles.role_from_caps`, `_DESCRIPTION_RULES`).**  Nutanix AHV, KVM and every Linux host with a software bridge send the capability set `B, W, R, S` (lldpd turns all four on); the word `W` made them access points, so nine hypervisor hosts of a data centre were drawn as `AP` in frames.  Now `B, W, R, S` is a server, a description with `Nutanix`, `AHV`, `VMware ESXi`, `Proxmox`, `XenServer` or `Hyper-V` is a server, and `B, W` or `W` alone is still an access point.  Access points outside a frame while their switch has one: 37 to 10; access points with more than one switch: 55 to 28.
- Measured on the 18 runs of 1.8 (the 11 of 1.8 and the 7 new ones, 158 pages): crossings 1,345 to 1,295 on the pages that have any, collinear overlay 20,378 px (same), lines through devices 41 (same), title hits 191 (same); nothing got worse except two crossings on three pages of the data-centre run, which now frames its servers.  The 20 stored packs and demo sets (288 pages): overlay 63,243 to 63,213 px, crossings 4,564 to 4,406, lines through devices 141 (same); the positions of all 60 stored layouts and all findings are unchanged.
- Tests: `tests/test_topology_v19.py` (28 tests: leaf switches of 9 random estates that 1.8 left 6 to 9 steps from their parent, the same over 120 estates, a core keeps its row of switches apart, the capability and description rules, the short member text, the SVG with and without the option, the version), synthetic captures only.  Each new rule was removed once to see a test fail.

### Topology Builder 1.8: the switches under a switch stand next to it in one big diagram, and a guessed cable from far above drops in by the way that crosses the fewest lines

Found on eleven runs of the same site set (a Jakarta office, a bank head office, a data-centre estate and a branch set, "One diagram" and "Auto").  In one big diagram every switch of a row shares the row with all the others, and the frames of end points are packed far apart; the child switches (the row below) kept the place the sweeps gave them while their parents were moved over their frames, so a child could stand 13 to 21 icon steps (up to 4,000 px) from the switch it hangs under, and the lines of its parent ran a long way across the page.  Measured per page on the same captures before and after.

- **Child switches follow their parent (`layout._arrange.centre_owners`).**  After the switches of a row have moved over their frames, a switch of the next row that has no frame of its own (a captured or only seen access switch, or anything with something under it) is wanted at the mean position of the switches above it that it is cabled to.  Only what stands farther than `FOLLOW_FAR` (6 icon steps) from them comes back, so a child that already stands near keeps its place (the pictures of the 20 stored packs are unchanged), and loose end points (phones, PCs, hosts) are left where the sweeps put them, because moving them lets a switch's comb run through them.
- **A guessed second cable takes the way in that crosses the fewest lines (`scene._route_into_frames`, `_crossings_of`).**  Every way in that touches no device (the straight line to a strip beside the target, or down from the source at `DROP_OFFSETS` 18, 105, 60 and 150 px to either side and along the top of the frame) is counted against the lines already drawn and the one with the fewest crossings wins, the first of equals.  When the source is more than `TALL_DROP` (260 px) above the frame the drops are tried before the straight line, which would be a long slant across every lane.
- Measured on the 11 runs (116 pages): crossings 5,348 to 736 (the one-diagram Overview of a 504-device office, 530 to 17; of a 19-floor set, 1,822 to a few hundred), lines through devices 27 to 27, collinear overlay 20,027 to 17,497 px, crowding 520,822 to 494,280, device names struck through 2,487 to 2,382; nothing got worse on any page.  The 20 stored packs and demo sets (288 pages): overlay 63,243 px (same), crossings 4,708 to 4,564, lines through devices 141 (same), positions of all 60 stored layouts and the findings unchanged.
- Tests: `tests/test_topology_v18.py` (11 tests on an invented estate of 12, 27 and 40 distribution switches: child distance under 6 steps against 20 to 78 before, children below their parent, nobody overlaps, a near child keeps its place, `_crossings_of`, drops beside the source or straight to the strip), synthetic captures only.  Each new rule was removed once to see a test fail.

### Topology Builder 1.7: lines that lay on each other are lifted apart, tall frames get room for their lines, access points of one switch stand in one frame

Found on nineteen runs of one site set (the floors of a Jakarta office, a bank head office and branches, a data centre) built with their own options, "Group end points" on and off.  Measured per page on the same captures before and after (the 19 runs: 205 pages; plus the 20 stored packs and the demo sets, 288 pages).

- **A last pass lifts lines that lie on each other (`scene._lift_apart`).**  Two lines whose straight runs (a vertical or horizontal piece between two corners) lie within 2 px of each other over more than 8 px look like one line.  The line that comes later (a comb line keeps its place) moves both corners of that run sideways by 8 px steps, to the first free track that touches no device; the pieces before and after it stay attached, so it only gets a slight slant at its ends.  Collinear overlap outside the icons: 219,384 px to 20,676 px on the 19 runs, 728,583 px to 63,243 px on the packs.
- **Tall frames get room.**  `layout.frame_pitch` widens the distance between the columns of a frame with many rows (up to `FRAME_PITCH_MAX` 1.9 icon steps) so that the strip between two columns holds `2 * (rows - 1)` channel lines `COMB_CHANNEL` apart; `_gaps` reserves the row height above a frame for the lanes of its comb (`COMB_LANE_ROOM` 7 px per lane, capped by `COMB_EXTRA_MAX`).  The channel of a short last row (centred, so between two columns of the full rows) uses the narrower strip (`_route_comb`, `ragged`).
- **A busy switch lets its lines leave one by one.**  Heads on one side of a switch with very many lines start `COMB_EXIT_MIN` (4 px) apart, the highest above the corner of the icon, instead of 7 px apart over a span too short for them (they lay on each other).
- **Channels are kept clear for the cores above (`layout._pin_channels`).**  Devices that stand between a core and its frame in an intermediate row no longer take the room the comb of the core needs to drop; where two cores pin the same row too tightly the row keeps its old order (`PIN_GAP_MIN`).  One floor's overlay went from 12,715 px to 0.
- **Access points of one switch stand in one frame.**  An access point with one cable that is read (CDP, LLDP, MAC table, port description) and others that are only guessed (ARP, routes) is framed under the switch of the read cable (`layout.box_groups`); the guessed line is no comb line of the switch that only guesses (`_route_comb`) and enters the frame by a strip (`_route_into_frames`).  Access points outside a frame while their switch has one: 30 to 18; switches with access points both framed and unframed: 12 to 0.
- **A guessed second cable finds a free way in.**  `_route_into_frames` tries the strips beside the target on both sides and one beyond each outer column, each straight or down from the source and along the top of the frame, and takes the first that touches no device (a line from one switch to an access point three floors away crossed three devices).  When no detour frees a line, `_detour_least` takes the path through the fewest devices instead of the 70 px slant.  Lines through devices: 93 to 89 on the 19 runs, 138 to 141 on the packs (the packs hold more bundle lines between sites, which no detour frees).
- **Names of frames keep off port labels.**  The name slides along the top strip to a gap that has neither a line nor a port label (`titles.end_title_spot(page, box, segments, rects)`; SVG and draw.io agree); port labels stay off the default place of the name (`labelplace`).  Frame names struck through by a line: 170 to 98; a frame name under a port label: 0.
- Known trade-off: a guessed second cable that now enters a frame adds crossings with the comb lines of the other switch (one floor 0 to 16); the crossings in all 19 runs went 2,207 to 2,220 (+0.6 %), on the packs 4,728 to 4,708.
- Tests: `tests/test_topology_v17.py` (35 tests: frame pitch, lanes, heads, channel pins, framing of access points, guessed cables, `_lift_apart` on staged runs, the version), synthetic captures only.  Each new rule was removed once to see a test fail.

### Topology Builder: access points under the switch they are plugged into, frame names off the lines, lines further apart

Found on three Sunter packs (a Cisco C3850 core over Huawei S5735 access switches with Cisco access points).  CDP frames go to the multicast address 01-00-0c-cc-cc-cc.  A Huawei switch does not run CDP, so it floods them, and the Cisco switch above it listed every access point behind the Huawei switch on its uplink port as if it were plugged in there.  The picture drew each access point twice (once on the Huawei switch from its LLDP table, once on the Cisco switch), which gave every access point two attachments, so none of them could be framed under its switch, and a Huawei switch that was not captured collected the access points of its neighbours.

- **Neighbours seen through a switch (`through.py`).** On a port with two or more neighbours, the neighbour learned by LLDP is the real cable (LLDP is link-local and never forwarded); without LLDP it is the one network device on the port.  Neighbours only CDP reported next to it are behind it: an end point that is plugged in somewhere else keeps that cable and loses the line through the port, one that is not is attached to the real neighbour (a single-sided link noted `seen through`).  No vendor name is involved.  New finding TOPO-046 (info) says what was moved; TOPO-011 is no longer raised for these ports and TOPO-045 does not count them as cables.
- **A device reported under its MAC.** A name that is a prefix and a 12-digit MAC (`NPOINT069F81027403`) now resolves to the device that the next switch reports by that MAC (`mac_in_name`, in `graph.build`, so stored packs benefit too).
- **Access points framed by default.** With "Collapse access points" off, two or more access points on one switch are framed under it; this now holds on the Huawei packs because every access point has one attachment.
- **Lines that lay on each other.** Two cables from one hub to two boxes that go round the same box took the same corners on lane 0 and ran together for hundreds of pixels; a lane whose legs another line uses is skipped (`scene._route_around_boxes`).
- **Frame names kept off the lines.** The name of an end-point frame slides along the top strip to a gap between lines (`titles.end_title_spot`; SVG and draw.io agree).
- **Comb lines further apart.** `COMB_LANE` 6 to 8 px, `COMB_CHANNEL` 5 to 8 px (narrowed to fit the strip between two columns, never under 5), `COMB_EXIT` 5 to 7 px, and a side with very many lines leaves along up to 52 px of the icon instead of 28.  The piece from the middle of the icon to its side lies under the icon, so two older tests that required it never to go up now start at the first waypoint.
- Measured on the demo and the 20 real packs, 288 pages against v47: collinear overlay 1,455,975 px to 728,583 px, crowded parallel lines (2.5 to 7 px apart) 7.84 M to 5.63 M px, frame titles hit by a line 554 to 293, crossings 4,844 to 4,728, lines through an icon 162 to 138; no page got worse in any of these.

### Topology Builder: Huawei captures read as completely as Cisco CDP / LLDP

A Huawei switch capture (`display lldp neighbor`, `display eth-trunk`, `display interface`) drew its neighbours with no address, no vendor and sometimes the wrong tier, and an Eth-Trunk was never checked against the other end.  Found on a real S5735 stack (access points, a Cisco core and an IP phone on its ports); fixed in the engine for every Huawei switch, not for that network.

- **Management address of a neighbour.** YunShan OS prints `Management address :10.x`, older VRP prints `Management address value :10.x`; both are read, so a neighbour that nobody captured now shows its address (an access point, the core) instead of a bare name.
- **System description.** The multi-line `System description` (it ends at the next field line, with or without a `System capabilities` line after it) is kept for every LLDP source that prints one, Cisco and Huawei.  The vendor and the role of a neighbour nobody captured are read from it (`names.vendor_from_description`, `roles.role_from_description`: `Cisco AP Software, ap1g7-k9w8` is an access point, a Huawei, Fortinet, Palo Alto, Aruba, Juniper, Arista, MikroTik, Ruckus or Ubiquiti banner names its vendor).  A name or a model rule still wins; the description only fills what they left empty.  Two WIPS access points that were drawn as access switches are access points now, and TOPO-004 no longer lists them as unknown network devices.
- **Eth-Trunk working mode.** `Working Mode: Normal` is a manual bundle, `Static` is LACP with a fixed configuration, `Dynamic` is LACP negotiated.  The trunk keeps the word `Eth-Trunk` as its protocol and adds `lacp_mode`, the state of each member (`Selected`, `Unselect`, `Individual`, as the LACP flags P, D, I) and the partner system ID.  The topology labels the bundle `4x1G LACP` (was `4x1G Eth-Trunk`), shows the member state, and TOPO-040 (the two ends disagree on the protocol) can now compare a Huawei end with a Cisco end: a Cisco LACP bundle against a Huawei `Normal` trunk is reported, against `Static` or `Dynamic` it is not.  The Switch Analyzer shows the mode next to each member port.
- **Speed and duplex.** `display interface` (long form) gives `Speed:` and `Duplex: FULL, Negotiation: ENABLE`, and the neighbour's `OperMau :speed (1000) /duplex (Full)` gives the far end; TOPO-025 (link below its rating) and TOPO-026 (two ends on different duplex) work for Huawei ports.
- The 20 real packs and the Cisco-only and mixed Cisco / Huawei captures give the same pictures, links and findings as before; only a Huawei-only build changes, as described.

### Credential leaks found by a token-level scan of real captures

A stricter scan (every credential value in 92 real captures, looked for as an exact string in each analyzer's answer, the Topology build and its downloads) found five places the first secret sweep did not reach. All are fixed and have a regression test that fails without the fix.

- **Wireless Center** showed WLAN pre-shared keys in clear text (`security wpa psk set-key ascii 0 <key>` in the profile tables, the relationship view and the exports). Raw `config` command lists are now passed through `redact_line` before anything is built from them; labels such as `WPA-PSK` are unchanged. Test: `tests/test_wireless_secrets.py`.
- **`redact_line` left the value visible after a Huawei `cipher` keyword.** `local-user x password irreversible-cipher <hash>` came out as `password <hidden> <hash>`. It now knows `cipher` / `irreversible-cipher` / `simple` (including `shared-key cipher`, `pass-phrase cipher`, `usm-user ... cipher`, `ospf authentication-mode md5 1 cipher`), Cisco WLC `psk set-key ascii|hex [0|8]`, `message-digest-key N md5 [type]` and `authentication-key N md5`. Key ids and `key chain` are left alone.
- **Switch Analyzer** raw lists `aaa_commands`, `ntp_commands`, `logging_commands` and `spanning_tree_commands` (NTP authentication keys, local-user hashes, TACACS/RADIUS keys) and the ACL "referenced by" lists (`snmp-server community NAME RO acl`, `enable secret ...`) are redacted.
- **Router Analyzer** object cross-reference (`used_by`, `applied`) is redacted the same way.
- Tests: `tests/test_analyzer_secret_leaks.py`, `tests/test_wireless_secrets.py`. Not covered, by nature: a password typed on its own line after an interactive prompt in a hand-written draft file, and free text in a comment ("the default password was set to ...").

### Convert Switch: hide passwords and keys

- New option on the Convert Switch page, **Hide passwords and keys in the output** (off by default). The converter has always kept the values it copies from the source (local-user hashes in the Huawei `REVIEW-PASSWORD-HASH` and `SOURCE-PASSWORD` lines, SNMP communities, shared keys) because a migration engineer needs them to paste a working configuration; that also makes the output a credential file. With the option on, `converter_engine/secret_mask.py` replaces every recognised value by `<hidden>` on the screen, in the download and in a batch ZIP. Two layers: command patterns (`secret 9 X`, `cipher X`, `key 7 X`, `snmp-server community X`, `snmp-agent community read cipher X`, SNMPv3 `auth`/`priv` keys, ProVision `password manager user-name ...`) and a sweep for the exact values found in the source, so a value copied into a REVIEW comment or a pass-through line is hidden too. Free-text lines (`description`, `remark`, `location`) and the converter's own `<PLACEHOLDER>` words are left alone.
- A notice above the result says how many lines carry a credential and whether they are hidden (`result.secrets`).
- Found on real Jalin captures: `enable secret` and `enable password` have no Huawei or Aruba equivalent and were passed on as REVIEW comments with the hash in them; with the option on they are hidden like the rest.
- Tests: `tests/test_convert_secret_mask.py` (about 60 checks on invented CANARY credentials in `tests/fixtures/secrets/`: six cross-vendor pairs, command shapes, ordinary commands left alone, route and download). Each command pattern was removed in turn: all but one duplicate are needed.

### Topology Builder: core, distribution and access as rows under the core, parents over their children

- Before, every switch whose name or model did not say "core" landed in one row, so a core with four neighbors was drawn as five boxes side by side with long lines jumping over each other. - Roles are found in two ways. **Host names**: the words `SWCO` / `SWCODI` / `SWCORE` / `CSW` (core), `SWDI` / `SWDS` / `SWDIST` / `DSW` (distribution) and `SWAC` / `SWAS` / `SWACC` / `ASW` (access) now count next to `CORE`, `DS`, `DIST`, `ACC` and the others. **Shape**: for switches whose name and model say nothing, a group of three or more linked switches has its core found from the graph (linked to a router, firewall or load balancer, else the middle of the group, else the most links; a tie between unrelated switches leaves the group alone), and from the core a switch with another switch below it is a distribution switch and one with none is an access switch. A role that the name gave is never changed. Layered design reference: https://www.cisco.com/c/en/us/td/docs/solutions/CVD/Campus/cisco-campus-lan-wlan-design-guide.html
- Lines from a switch to the switches below it leave out of the side of the icon, run along and drop straight into the switch, each leaving at its own height along the side of the icon (20 px apart, so every port name has room and none sits on another line), so they no longer cross the name written under the icon or each other. A switch right below its parent, a switch with two parents and a line that would hit another device keep the ordinary line.
- A row is centred over its children only when the devices in it have no lines to the rows below that other things decide; routers and the like keep their places, so large sets laid out around a router do not change.
- Measured as line crossings on the page: the four Cisco captures stay at 0 and now have no long line that jumps along a row; the larger backup set 191 to 88; the Cisco LAN sample 32 to 26; the multi-site sample unchanged.

### Topology Builder: neighbors that share one management address stay separate devices (TOPO-030)

- Fixed: on Cisco captures whose LLDP neighbors were several different switches, the Topology Builder drew one neighbor instead of several, although Switch Analyzer listed every neighbor correctly. Cause: the identity step falls back to the management address when the serial, chassis MAC and name do not match, and a group of access switches (here Huawei S5731 units) all advertised the same management address (`192.168.1.253`, a factory or copied default) with different names and chassis IDs. The first one that was seen took the address and every other neighbor, in the same capture and in other captures, was merged into it.
- Now an address that two or more differently named neighbors advertise is never used to match a device (identity, inferred neighbors, routed links). Those neighbors are matched by serial, chassis MAC and name only, so each keeps its own node and its own links. A neighbor that has no name (its chassis MAC stands in) does not count as a second name, and one device written in two name forms (FQDN, or a `(SERIAL)` suffix) is still one device.
- New finding **TOPO-030** (medium): "management address advertised by more than one device", with the address and the names. The same address on several switches is normally a default or copied address; each switch needs its own. Reference: IEEE 802.1AB (LLDP), https://standards.ieee.org/ieee/802.1AB/6047/ .
- Checked on four real Cisco Catalyst 3650 captures (core and distribution switches with Huawei and Cisco access neighbors): every neighbor row in the CDP/LLDP tables is now a separate link. On a larger backup set, seven neighbors that were merged are separate nodes (96 to 103); sets without shared addresses give the same result as before.

### Convert Switch: PoE, Aruba 2540 (ArubaOS-Switch) and banners, found by converting every sample backup

- **PoE is written only where the source set it.** Every conversion to Cisco or Aruba CX wrote `power inline never` / `no poe-enable` on every port (uplinks and VLAN interfaces included) because "PoE not mentioned" was read as "PoE off"; that would have cut power to phones and access points. A source without a PoE statement now gets none, a source that switches PoE off keeps it on that port only, and a Huawei target gets `undo poe enable` on a PoE copper port (or a `REVIEW-POE` line where the target's PoE capability is not known).
- **A `show tech` capture of an Aruba 2540 no longer becomes the configuration.** The block now ends at the next bare `show ...` command; before, a 49,000-line capture was taken as a 31,000-line configuration and every line was a review item (the Cisco and Aruba targets). The switch capture reader also keeps the text of a banner line that starts with "Show ..." as written.
- **ArubaOS-Switch (2540 / 2930 / 5400) statements that were dropped are converted:** `interface <port> / disable` (the port stays shut), `name "..."` (description), the IP address and `ip helper-address` written inside `vlan <id>` (the management address was lost), and a port tagged in one VLAN and untagged in a later one stays a trunk with the untagged VLAN as native (it was turned into an access port that dropped the tagged VLAN; a port untagged first and tagged later lost its native VLAN from the allowed list). `exit`, `; ` comment lines, `module N type ...` and `no untagged` are no longer review items; `ip address dhcp-bootp` is now read as a DHCP client (see below).
- **Banners keep their kind and their text.** A wrapped ArubaOS-Switch banner (`banner motd` on its own line, one quoted string with `\n` escapes) is one banner instead of fifteen review lines; `banner exec` / `banner login` are written back as `exec` / `login` (they all became `banner motd`; on Huawei, exec is `header shell information`).
- **Less noise in the review list:** `end`, the `version` stamp and `no ip address` are not review items for Cisco and Aruba targets; an Aruba source no longer gets the Cisco CDP / "LLDP disabled" notes.
- **Fixed speed / duplex and DHCP-client addresses are carried instead of listed as unsupported.** `speed 100` + `duplex full` (Cisco), `speed-duplex 100-full` (ArubaOS-Switch) and Huawei `speed` / `duplex` are read and written on every target: Cisco `speed N` / `duplex D`, AOS-CX `speed 100-full` / `speed 1000-full` / `speed 10g`, Huawei `undo negotiation auto` + `speed N` on a copper GE port. Each fixed port carries a `REVIEW-SPEED` line because the far end has to be fixed the same way (one side fixed, the other auto-negotiating, gives a duplex mismatch). Combinations a target has no single command for (a fixed 1000 Mbps copper port on Huawei, `speed-duplex auto-100`) stay review lines. `ip address dhcp` (Cisco), `ip address dhcp-bootp` / `ip dhcp` (ArubaOS-Switch / AOS-CX) and `ip address dhcp-alloc` (Huawei) are one DHCP-client flag: written back as `ip address dhcp`, `ip dhcp` or `ip address dhcp-alloc` (+ `dhcp enable`). AOS-CX takes `ip dhcp` only on the management interface and VLAN 1, so any other interface gets a `REVIEW-DHCP-CLIENT` line.
- Checked on the 86 sample switch captures converted to Huawei, Cisco and Aruba (258 runs, no failure but the one capture without a running-config, which is refused as before); review items fell from 92,000 to 21,100.

### Topology Builder 1.5: access points framed under their switch with or without the end-point option, unrelated switches kept apart, lanes of neighbouring switches separated

- **Access points keep their frame when "Collapse access points into one node per switch" is off.** Frames were tied to the option "Group end points under the same switch", so drawing access points one by one while end points (phones, PCs, servers) were not grouped scattered the access points. `Graph.group_aps` (set by the service from the access-point option) frames the access points of one switch (two or more) even when `group_endpoints` is off; phones, PCs and servers stay unframed in that case. `layout.framed_roles(graph)` returns the roles a frame may hold: all end-point roles, only `ap`, or none.

### Topology Builder 1.4: no more hooks into end points, two lines never share the way into one device, captures stay cached

- **End-point lines no longer hook round their column.** In a frame with an even number of columns there is no middle column, so the lines to the two middle columns (and to the rows below them) had no channel between the switch's label and the column: they ran past the end point on the far strip and came back into it from the outside, a hook. The switch now stands over one of the two middle columns (the one nearer to where it stood; `layout._arrange.centre_owners` moves its wish by half a column, also for a switch that is alone in its row). That column becomes the middle column: its first end point keeps the straight line, the rows under it drop beside the column on the switch's own side, and every other column has room for its inner channel. On 60 random sets (1 to 6 switches, up to 45 end points each) the lines that used the far strip fell from 149 of 846 to 0 of 938 (the 1.2 limit "about 10 %" is gone); on the real 7-switch, 93-host set none is left. A row that was centred is no longer laid out again from the row above (it was, when the switch above it had been moved too).
- **Two lines of different switches into one device no longer lie on each other.** An access point or host that two switches report (a dual-homed AP, a host on two uplinks) got two lines that arrived along the same vertical or horizontal run. The line that belongs to the device's own frame keeps the run, every other one moves its last corner sideways by `APPROACH_STEP` (14 px, alternately left and right; `scene._untangle_approaches`), so the last stretch is a slight slant. This closes the one overlap that was left on the multi-site overview (a real 24-site set, Automatic grouping: now 0 overlaps and 0 lines through foreign frames on all 24 pages). The in-frame line that the 1.3 audit counted there was this same line: it ends in a device of that frame, so it has to enter it.
- **Captures stay cached.** The reading cache kept 24 captures; a set of 24 or more files evicted its own first files while it was read, so every new build (another option, another view, another SD-WAN site) read all of them again (17 to 30 s). It now keeps 400 (a read device is about 50 KB: 20 MB at most), and the second build of the same captures takes no reading time at all. The first read is still bound by the Switch Analyzer's parsers (about 30 s for 10 large captures); that code is shared with the analyzers and was not changed.
- **Not changed, on purpose.** Forcing one diagram ("None") on 24 sites puts every frame of end points in one row (19 frames, about 26,000 px): use Automatic or a site grouping, which gives one page per site and an overview of the boxes.

### Topology Builder 1.3: switches over their frames, lines that keep to their own switch, a tighter overview of large sites

- **The lines of one switch no longer run on the lines of another.** Access switches used to sit between the core and the core's frame of end points, so the comb lines of the core passed through the access row and lay on the access switches' own lines. After the rows are placed every switch with a frame of end points is now centred over its frame (`layout._arrange.centre_owners`: one pass over frozen frame centres, per-pair gaps in `_spread`, a channel `DROP_ROOM` wide kept free for a switch whose frame is more than one row below). One pass is deliberate: an iterative version let owners and frames push each other and widened some pages four-fold. Star pages (routers on top) keep their sweep order. On a real 7-switch, 93-host set the stretches where the lines of two switches lie on each other fell from 82 to 0, on 60 random multi-switch sets from 81 to 0.
- **Lines keep out of frames that are not theirs.** A line between two devices that do not belong to a frame now treats that frame as an obstacle and bends round it (`scene._frames`, `_detour`; the wider lifts of 460 and 620 px apply only when a frame is in the way, so star and router pages keep their v1.2 routes). - Known limits: one site-level line to an unframed access point that lies inside another switch's frame area can still cross that frame (1 of about 1,100 segments on the largest real set); a one-diagram page with many floors is still very wide; the far-strip hook of v1.2 remains on about 10 % of the lines; analysing 100 or more captures is dominated by the parsers (about 30 s), not the layout.

### Topology Builder 1.2: the comb on dense frames and curved pages, port labels on their own drops, bridges instead of V peaks, a clean BIG-IP map

- **Dense frames (a core with dozens of hosts, two neighbouring access switches) are combed too.** A device that stands right beside a switch (the second switch of a pair, a core next to an aggregation) used to stop the comb: a drop ran into its box and the whole switch fell back to diagonals. The comb now measures the free band between the switch and the first device beside it (`scene._comb_wall`) and squeezes its drops into that band (down to 2 px apart when a wall limits them, 4 px otherwise), and the far strip of a column is only taken when the squeeze is not enough. On the real 7-switch, 93-host set 187 of 188 end-point lines are combed; the two pages that used to show V peaks and lines across a neighbour's box (two access switches side by side, 24 and 26 hosts) are fully orthogonal and clear of every box.
- **Curved line shape keeps the comb.** 1.0 and 1.1 skipped the comb on curved pages. The lines of a frame now run the same comb, drawn with rounded corners (SVG: quarter-round joins; draw.io: `rounded=1;arcSize=12` with every waypoint), while links that bend once are still drawn as the single smooth curve.
- **The port label at the switch stands on the line's own drop.** The lines of a comb are 3 to 5 px apart, so a label beside a line belonged to its neighbour. The label of the switch end is now centred on the vertical run (the drop) of its own line, one band lower for each line, so you can read which port belongs to which lane.
- **A link blocked by a device in the same row is a bridge, not a V.** The router used to peak the line up (one bend) over the device in between, which read like two links meeting at a point. It now leaves the first device, runs flat above the blocker and comes down into the second (two corners, each 60 px along the line from its own device). Curved pages keep the smooth single-point curve.
- **BIG-IP map: no line through the boxes, long names cut.** In the top blocks of the map the line of a virtual server in the second or third row ran across the boxes of the row below. Each such line now goes up the channel between the two blocks, along a lane in the gap under its own row and into the bottom of its box (the box farthest from the middle takes the lowest lane and the outermost trunk, so no two cross; checked for 26, 60 and 90 virtual servers). Names longer than their box are cut with an ellipsis (the full name stays in the tooltip / draw.io value). The lanes reach draw.io as waypoints.
- Checked: 60 random sets (1 to 3 switches, 2 to 40 hosts, lines written either way, 845 comb lines): none goes up, none reverses on its lane, all start at their first end; about 10 % use the far strip of their column (one hook). Not changed: the L3 - Logical and Route page of two aggregation switches with about 30 VLAN interfaces stays dense (use "group L3").

### Topology Builder 1.1: no more triangle lines to end points, drops for the rows under a switch, strips that never make a line turn back

### Topology Builder 1.0: end-point lines as a comb, Palo Alto high availability and Panorama exports, ports described twice

- **Lines to the end points of a switch run as a comb.** With end points drawn one by one and framed per switch, the cables used to leave the switch as long diagonals that crossed other frames and each other. ### Topology Builder 0.9: lines go around site boxes, one site per firewall chassis, Palo Alto aggregate-ethernet

- **Multi-site Overview: lines between two site boxes no longer run through a third box.** A line whose straight path crosses another site's frame is routed along the shortest path over the corners of the frames (a visibility graph with a small bend penalty), a few pixels off their edges. Lines between the same two boxes take separate lanes (five, 7 px apart) so a bundle fans out instead of lying on one line. ### Convert Firewall: guided conversion, PAN-OS reference checks, IKE/IPsec syntax fix

Guided conversion (MikroTik RouterOS to Palo Alto)
- New "Guided Conversion (step by step)" button on Convert Firewall. Ten steps in dependency order: Target, Interfaces, Zones, Routing, Objects, NAT, Security policy, VPN, Services, Review & export. Each step is checked when you press "Validate & continue"; the next step opens only when the step has no errors and its warnings are acknowledged. Download is refused until every step passes.
- Interfaces step: source port, detected role (INET from DHCP client, masquerade egress, default-route next hop or public address; LAN from DHCP server, bridge membership or private address), addresses and a target-port dropdown built from the target model. Swapping LAN and INET is two dropdown changes; zones, NAT egress, MSS, virtual router and IKE local interface follow. Source-side checks: an in-use port with no target (MAP-UNMAPPED), two sources on one port (MAP-DUP), a port the model lacks (MAP-PORT-MISSING), and a renumber list for the cutover (MAP-RENUMBER).
- Zones step: one zone per interface. A bridge member port is layer2 and takes its bridge's zone, so a renamed bridge zone also reaches rules written on a member port. A rule on a RouterOS interface list uses the zones of the list's members (`from [ A B ]` when they were split).
- VPN step: local interface per IKE gateway, zone and tunnel.N per tunnel.
- `converter_engine/wizard/pan_validator.py` reads the generated set commands back and reports what does not resolve or would not commit: undefined interfaces, zones, address objects, services, IKE/IPsec profiles and gateways; an interface in two zones; layer2/layer3 zone mismatch; overlapping subnets; ports beyond the target model; invalid names; NAT with several destination zones or `!` negation; IKE gateway without local interface; non-PAN-OS algorithm keywords; plus warnings (weak crypto, placeholder PSK, unreachable next hop, ECMP, shadowed and any-any rules, NAT egress without address). It cannot replace `validate full` on a firewall, and the export header says so.
- Sessions live under `uploads/configuration/wizard/<token>` and are removed after a day. API: `POST /configuration/api/wizard/start`, `POST /configuration/api/wizard/<token>/validate`, `GET /configuration/api/wizard/<token>/download`.

VLANs, bridges and disabled ports (Interfaces step)
- Every port, VLAN and bridge shows Enabled or Disabled from the source. A disabled physical port is written with `link-state down` and reviewed.
- New "VLAN and bridge interfaces" table. A VLAN on a bridge is either "vlan.N + tagged L2" (default) or an L3 sub-interface on a chosen port (router-on-a-stick); a VLAN on a port is an L3 sub-interface whose parent follows the port mapping. Tag and vlan.N unit are editable, and the resulting PAN-OS name is shown. A one-port bridge can be routed on its port instead of vlan.N.
- Checks: tag outside 1-4094 (MAP-VLAN-TAG), sub-interface without a mapped parent (MAP-VLAN-PARENT), two VLAN interfaces on one unit (MAP-VLAN-UNIT), two VLANs on one port.tag (MAP-VLAN-DUP), routed bridge with more than one port (MAP-BRIDGE-ROUTED); the validator adds IF-MODE for a port written as both layer2 and layer3 and checks that a sub-interface and its parent are the same layer.
- An interface named or commented WAN/ISP/INET/internet/uplink is detected as INET.
- An enabled port with nothing on it (no address, not in a bridge, no rule, NAT, DHCP or VLAN uses it) starts unmapped. "Not mapped (skip)" now really skips the port: the translator writes nothing for it (it used to auto-guess ethernet1/N and add an empty layer3 interface, a zone and a virtual-router entry) and leaves one review note. Unmapping a port that is in use is still an error.

Fixes from a debugging pass on the guided conversion
- Export could differ from the page: after an edit that was not re-checked, the stepper still opened Review as "Passed" and Download exported the last checked state. Leaving a step with unchecked edits now checks them first, and the download carries the revision of the state the page last saw checked (`?rev=`); the server refuses any other revision.
- A port in a slot the model does not have (`ethernet2/1` on a PA-505) passed. Wizard (MAP-PORT-MISSING) and validator (IF-PORT-MISSING) now check slot 1 and the port number.
- Two tunnels could be given the same tunnel.N and both bound to it without an error; a non-numeric unit silently became 1. New MAP-TUNNEL-UNIT / MAP-TUNNEL-DUP in the VPN step and VPN-TUN-DUP in the validator; an invalid unit writes no tunnel interface.
- Marking the WAN as an SD-WAN link lost the IKE gateway's local interface: the owner of the local address is now also looked up in the SD-WAN lines.
- The VPN step's local-interface list held physical ports only, so a WAN on a sub-interface (`ethernet1/1.100`), vlan.N or SD-WAN could not be picked and the error could not be fixed from the page. It now lists every layer3 interface in the output.
- A session that started more than a day ago was deleted by the next start even while in use. Every check now refreshes the session's age.

Translator fixes (all conversions, not only the wizard)
- A VLAN on a bridge was built as vlan.N with the bare bridge ports as members, while the same ports already belonged to the bridge's untagged VLAN object; PAN-OS refuses one interface in two VLAN objects. It now uses a tagged layer2 sub-interface (`ethernet1/2.20 tag 20`) on each bridge port.
- Synthetic vlan.N numbers for untagged bridges restart at 900 per conversion, so converting the same file twice gives the same output.
- IKE and IPsec now use the PAN-OS CLI paths, which follow the configuration XML: `network ike crypto-profiles ike-crypto-profiles`, `network ike crypto-profiles ipsec-crypto-profiles`, `network ike gateway`, and `auto-key proxy-id` under the tunnel. The old `network ike-gateway`, `network ike-crypto-profile`, `network ipsec-crypto-profile` and `proxy-id` without `auto-key` are rejected by PAN-OS.
- Encryption names are PAN-OS keywords (`aes-256` becomes `aes-256-cbc`); lifetimes use the largest exact unit (`lifetime days 1` instead of `lifetime-seconds 86400`, which is above the 65535 limit); `protocol version` is written; `peer-address ip` drops `/32`; `passive-mode` sits under `protocol-common`.

- A NAT rule whose RouterOS match used `!address` no longer writes the `!` into the PAN-OS address field (refused at commit even on a disabled rule); the rule stays disabled and reviewed.
- The PAN-OS set parser (Firewall Analyzer) reads both the new and the old IKE syntax, bracketed algorithm lists, `lifetime <unit> N`, gateway version/local-address/passive-mode, and `network interface tunnel units`.

### Easy start for new users: no login, self-installing start scripts, new README and PANDUAN

No login
- The shared-password login is gone. A fresh download opens straight to the dashboard: no password in the terminal, no `data/.auth_password`, no lockout. `core/auth.py` and `templates/login.html` are deleted; `/login` and `/logout` no longer exist. `NES_AUTH_PASSWORD` and `NES_DISABLE_AUTH` are ignored (an old `data/.auth_password` file does nothing and can be deleted).
- New `core/local_guard.py` replaces it with what a login-free localhost tool still needs. It answers only to this computer's names (`localhost`, `127.0.0.1`, `[::1]`; more via `NES_ALLOWED_HOSTS`), which stops DNS rebinding, and it refuses POST, PUT, PATCH and DELETE that another website started (`Sec-Fetch-Site` other than same-origin/none, or an `Origin` that is not the page's own host), which stops cross-site request forgery against routes that take device addresses and SSH credentials. curl, scripts and the test client send neither header and are not affected. `NES_ALLOW_REMOTE=1` turns the Host check off and keeps the cross-site check.
- `run.py`: the outdated "no authentication" comment is now true again and the messages are plain language. Starting Keystone while it already runs reopens the browser instead of failing; a port held by another program says how to pick another one (`NES_PORT`); a non-numeric `NES_PORT` is explained.
- `verify_install.py` no longer logs in; it checks that a foreign Host header is refused.

Start scripts
- `start_windows.bat` and `start_mac_linux.sh` install Keystone on the first start (a missing `.venv` runs the setup), so one double-click or one command is enough.
- `setup_windows.bat` looks for Python 3.13, 3.12, 3.11, then any 3.11 or newer (`py` launcher, then `python`), and says where to download it when none is found. Both setup scripts fall back to `requirements.txt` when the pinned `requirements-lock.txt` does not install, and print what to try when setup fails (proxy, antivirus, `python3-venv` on Debian or Ubuntu). The macOS/Linux scripts are started with `bash ...` so a ZIP download without the executable bit works. `stop_*` honour `NES_PORT` and say "Keystone", not "NES".
- New `.gitattributes`: `*.sh` is stored and checked out with LF, `*.bat` and `*.vbs` with CRLF (a shell script with CRLF fails on macOS and Linux with `env: 'bash\r': No such file or directory`).

Documentation
- README.md and docs/PANDUAN.md: three-step Quick start at the top, what to expect on the first start, a Start and stop table, how to update, Windows SmartScreen and antivirus notes, a longer Troubleshooting table, and a short "Is it safe without a login?" explanation. docs/PRD.md: security rule and architecture diagram updated.
